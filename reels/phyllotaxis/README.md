# Phyllotaxis

**Wetware** · a shoot apex placing organs where the ones already there object least

<img src="loop.webp" width="320" align="right">

> *The plant is not counting. You are.*

One rule, repeated once per plastochrone: look round the rim, start the next
primordium at whatever angle is furthest — in the inhibitory sense — from the
primordia already down; then let the tissue underneath expand, which carries
everything outwards and clears the rim for the next one.

The spiral counts come out at 8, 13, 21 near the core and 21, 34, 55 at the rim,
and the measured median divergence is 137.37°. **Neither number is in the rule.**
Constant density forces radius to go as the square root of age, which makes the
head geometrically self-similar.

What the divergence settles to is a property of the parameter regime rather than
a theorem. At the apex size used here the placement locks into an ordered window
and lands within a seventh of a degree of the golden angle; at other apex sizes
it locks onto different fractions — near five thirteenths of a turn at one
setting, which throws the organs into thirteen separate arms — and above about
1.4 spacings it stops settling at all. Which window a setting falls in can only
be found by running it and measuring.

Colour is age, on a violet-to-magenta ramp with a cyan core. The youngest organ
is in the middle, which runs the palette the opposite way round from most of the
account: the bright end is the centre and the rim is the oldest thing in frame.

```
shoot apical meristem  ·  Douady & Couder 1992
grow · inhibit · place the next one

```
