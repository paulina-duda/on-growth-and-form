# Defect

**Substrate** · an organism's own parts, with the organism taken away

<img src="loop.webp" width="320" align="right">

> *Nothing here is alive. It still cannot rest.*

A film of microtubules one filament thick, a molecular motor that walks along
them, and the fuel that motor runs on. No cell, no membrane, no genome, and
nothing anywhere in it that could be called a decision.

The filaments line up with their neighbours — that is the whole of what
*nematic* means. Kinesin walks along one filament while holding another, so
neighbours slide past each other, and a row of filaments sliding along its own
direction pushes fluid along that direction. **Alignment is turned into flow.**

Then the flow bends the alignment that made it. There is no activity level at
which an aligned film is stable against itself, so it always buckles, and a bend
that keeps growing cannot stay a bend. Where the alignment breaks, the direction
turns by half a turn around a point: a topological defect, +½ or −½. Halves
cannot exist alone, so they arrive in pairs. The +½ has a head like a comet and
swims; the −½ has three arms and mostly sits. When the two meet they annihilate
and that patch of film is whole again. Counted well inside the drop at the end
of the clip: 232 of one sign against 234 of the other.

It never arrives anywhere — not order, not disorder, but a fixed rate of tearing
that will keep going for as long as there is ATP in the dish.
